# AI usage

I used AI tools while building this. They sped the research workflow up; they did not supply the
evidence.

## Where AI helped

- identifying suitable official sources and likely point-in-time failure modes;
- scaffolding parsers, validation tests and documentation;
- proposing alternative chart layouts and tightening explanatory language;
- drafting independent source, pipeline, report and adversarial-review workstreams in parallel;
- suggesting scientific mechanisms and external-validity questions for human verification;
- adversarial review for causality, unit, licensing and look-ahead claims;
- checking that the final narrative distinguishes observations from interpretation;
- identifying a rights-minimal publication boundary for upstream artifacts.

## What stayed deterministic

- source downloads and SHA-256 hashes;
- parsing and unit handling for machine-readable source fields;
- sanitization of publication-calendar facts and the machine-readable price extracts;
- managed-money net calculations;
- expanding prior-only signal thresholds and availability-time cooldowns;
- public-proxy event returns, censoring and deterministic block-bootstrap statistics;
- physical-balance revisions, regional-grinding comparisons and weather diagnostics;
- call-spread payoff, breakeven, maximum-loss sizing and scenario tables;
- peak, trough, latest and percentage-change calculations;
- effective-time and available-time filtering;
- chart data and every displayed metric;
- reproducibility and integrity tests.

All of that is implemented in Python or workbook formulas. ICCO balance, regional-grinding and
scientific-registry rows are human-reviewed factual transcriptions, not model-generated
observations. Tests fail when derived data, headline assertions, timing rules, required report
text or links diverge. Report bytes are deterministic across repeated builds in the same
environment; they are not claimed to be byte-identical across operating systems and font stacks.

## Human controls

Official source pages and downloaded artifacts are the evidence of record. Numerical claims are
checked against deterministic outputs, and interpretive language is reviewed for overstatement.

I verify every model-suggested quotation, citation, observation or estimate against the
underlying source. AI output is never treated as a market-data source.

The public repository does not include the full World Bank workbook or CFTC page-wrapper bytes.
Official URLs and upstream hashes preserve provenance, while deterministic extracts contain only
the facts needed to reproduce the study.

Scientific papers remain a human-reviewed evidence registry, not model-generated signals. AI
helped propose hypotheses and tests; the frozen deterministic rule produced an inconclusive
result, and I kept that result rather than tuning it away.

No model decides, routes or executes a trade. Risk sizing is a transparent hypothetical formula
applied to clearly illustrative inputs. There is no broker connection, order object or automated
trading action.
